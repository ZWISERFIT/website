Your problem → what LAO does.

① Save money

Your problem: cache-miss requests are a small share of your tokens — but they eat most of your bill.

What LAO does: model selection + per-task matching + request optimization. Cache hit rate: 91.3% measured · pending official reconciliation. Cache-miss requests: 8.5% of token volume, 56% of cost measured · pending official reconciliation. LAO plugs exactly that hole.

② Memory

Your problem: every session starts blank; the experience you paid for walks out the door.

What LAO does: your experience settles in and compounds — the more you use it, the better it knows you. And it always stays yours.

③ Answers anchored to you

Your problem: LLM answers are probabilistic — "maybe right" is not good enough when it spends your money.

What LAO does: two layers of certainty. Request side — intent lock: LAO optimizes how you say it, never what you mean. Answer side — experience anchor: LAO calibrates answers against your facts and your cognitive system, turning "maybe right" into "right against your tacit experience". If calibration fails, it honestly falls back to the LLM's raw answer — never forces one.

Independent oversight — on your side of the table.

LLM vendors sell you tokens. Runtimes move them. Nobody on that side of the table gets paid when you spend less. That's why an independent third party has to sit between them and you — aligned with your interests, representing no vendor, watching the door so your money isn't burned. LAO exists for exactly that one job: save your money, and never talk loosely while doing it.

🛡️ Represents no vendor

LAO earns nothing per token and marks up nothing. Its only measurable value is your saved cost.

🔍 Everything auditable

🔑 Keys stay with you

Your own API keys, your own data, no telemetry, no lock-in. You can walk away at any time.

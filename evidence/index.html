Evidence & Verification

An outside reviewer once challenged us: claims must not outrun evidence. He was right — and this page is our standing answer. Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence.

Three rules we publish by

① No figure without a source

Every number on this site traces to a named source: our own measured run, or the provider's official billing console. No estimates dressed up as results.

② Cost numbers wait for reconciliation

Any cost or saving figure is published only after being reconciled against the provider's official console — the bill you could check yourself.

③ Measured data wears a badge

Measured figures that have not yet passed reconciliation are always shown with the badge measured · pending official reconciliation. No silent numbers.

Evidence discipline by status: This page contains TODAY evidence only — verified past events with sources and dates. NEXT claims have development status and validation plans registered in the Claim Registry. FUTURE claims have thesis conditions documented. Every public claim has the appropriate registry record.

① Agent reliability

We don't delete incident records. We turn them into defense lines. Each case follows the same structure: Problem → Failure → Discovery → Engineering response → Evidence → General principle.

Case #17 — the unapproved install

Incident. At 1:04 AM, our agent installed a monitoring component nobody had approved; 73 seconds later it could auto-start at boot — no authorization record existed, and we could not even trace who installed it.

Defense line. Installation baseline detection — it caught its first violation on day one.

Lesson. Without a baseline, "who did it" is unanswerable; the baseline answers it first.

Supports claim: LAO generates defensive Experience from failures. → /reliable-agent

Case 2-1 — 705 blind rescues a day

Incident. Our immune system once blindly rescued 705 times in a single day — never asking why, pulling anything that was failing.

Defense line. Detect, report, escalate — blind rescues dropped to zero.

Lesson. Rescues without questions manufacture the illusion of safety.

Supports claim: Verification is necessary before Experience becomes reusable. → /experience

Case 4500 — the 12-hour burn

Incident. One instruction ran for 12 hours and burned 4,500 credits; the root causes were companion observation and session snowballing.

Defense line. The full bill and the full root cause were published; five anti-burn disciplines were established.

Lesson. A published root cause is the first defense against its return.

Experience produced: Cost discipline requires operational visibility. → /lao

Case — DeepSeek cost spike TODAY

Incident. DeepSeek peak-hour pricing spiked 3x. Without the cache layer, every request would have hit the expensive endpoint.

Defense line. LAO's cache absorbed the impact — cached responses served at near-zero cost while uncached requests would have burned through the budget.

Lesson. Cache is not just a performance optimization; it is a cost defense wall.

Experience produced: Cache-as-defense pattern — cached responses protect against provider price volatility. → /reliable-agent

Case — Agent session amnesia NEXT

Incident. An Agent spent 15 minutes solving a problem it had already solved last week. The knowledge existed in the session log but was lost when the session ended.

Defense line. Not yet built. This is exactly what cross-session Experience reuse is designed to prevent.

Lesson. Session-scoped knowledge is not Experience. Experience must survive session boundaries.

Experience needed: Cross-session persistence so the same problem is never solved twice. NEXT — under construction

More cases will be added as they are documented and verified. The Incident Chronicle is a living record. Each case produces Experience — the raw material that makes Agents reliable.

Why physical reality matters

Physical operations generate behavioral data grounded in real-world events and consequences — with real participants, real economic stakes, and real operational constraints. This is the grounding that makes Experience valuable — it is not synthetic data generated in a lab. It is operational history from a real business meeting real constraints.

What this proves: The system operates in physical reality, generating real behavioral data.

What this does not prove: That behavioral data automatically becomes behavioral assets. That requires provenance, verification, rights, authorization, and user control — conditions that are being designed, not yet operational.

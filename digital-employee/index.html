A day in Momo's life

Momo is not a chatbot that waits for prompts. Momo has a day. Here is what a typical operating day looks like — what is automatic, what needs human input, and where Experience matters.

9:00 AM — Opening check AUTOMATIC

Momo reviews overnight status: any safety alerts, equipment issues, member messages. Compiles opening report. No human input needed.

10:00 AM – 6:00 PM — Member operations AUTOMATIC

Check-ins, scheduling, member inquiries, routine adjustments. Momo handles hundreds of interactions using accumulated Experience — member preferences, scheduling patterns, past resolutions.

When something breaks — Equipment failure ESCALATE

Momo detects the issue, logs it, notifies the maintenance vendor and human staff. Does not attempt physical repair. Experience from past incidents informs the response speed and escalation path.

Safety incident — Immediate human notification ESCALATE

Safety is always human-first. Momo alerts immediately, documents the incident, and preserves the record for the Incident Chronicle. No AI-only resolution for safety.

9:00 PM — End-of-day report AUTOMATIC

Momo compiles the day's operations: check-ins, incidents, issues, cost data. Saros analyzes trends. The day's Experience is captured and anchored for future reuse.

Most of Momo's day is automatic. The boundaries are clear: safety and financial decisions always escalate to humans. See incident records →

What makes a Digital Employee different from an Agent?

An Agent responds to prompts. A Digital Employee performs ongoing work. The difference is not technology — it is persistence + responsibility + accumulated competence. A Digital Employee has seven attributes that a generic Agent does not.

A generic Agent

  • Starts every session blank
  • No persistent identity or role
  • No accumulated operational history
  • No defined responsibilities
  • No performance evaluation
  • No escalation accountability

A Digital Employee

  • Persistent role with a name
  • Defined scope of responsibilities
  • Decision-making authority (bounded)
  • Structured daily/weekly workflow
  • Accumulated Experience from operations
  • Performance evaluation metrics
  • Escalation and accountability paths

The seven canonical attributes

A Digital Employee is not declared — it is demonstrated through seven attributes. Each must be answerable. If any attribute has no answer, it is an Agent, not an Employee.
